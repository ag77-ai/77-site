How to run a session — sets, order and rest

STRAIGHT SETS. Finish every set of one exercise before you move to the next one. Not a circuit. Not one set of each and go round again.

So the morning of Day 1 runs like this, in this exact order:

  Scapular pull-up — set 1 · rest 45s · set 2 · rest 45s · set 3 · rest 45s · set 4
  then move on
  Chin-up — set 1 · rest 120s · set 2 · rest 120s · set 3
  then move on
  Negative pull-up — set 1 · rest 120s · set 2 · rest 120s · … · set 5
  then move on … and so on to the end.

Why straight sets and not a circuit: you are still learning these patterns under load. A circuit means every set after the first is performed tired, and a tired rep of a pattern you have not mastered teaches your body the wrong thing. Straight sets keep every rep clean.

The rest is now written on every single line. You never have to guess. The logic behind it:

  120 sec — pull-ups, chin-ups, negatives. These build strength and skill. Your max is 5. Rushing them just makes you worse at them.
  90 sec — the blue TECHNICAL lift. One per session. Perfect reps.
  75 sec — presses, rows, squats, dips, lunges. The main work.
  40–45 sec — raises, flys, calves, and all abs. Short on purpose.

DENSITY — and an honest correction. Day 1 afternoon ran 81 minutes with 41 of them below 117 bpm, against your own unplanned session two days earlier at 61 minutes and an average of 137. I first read that as wasted rest. You told me you were watching the form videos between sets so you would not butcher the posture on movements you had never done. That is the correct trade and you should keep doing it. Learning the pattern beats rushing it, and week one and two are supposed to be slow. From roughly week three, once the patterns are yours, the timer takes over and the same work should fit in 60 minutes at a far higher average. Density is the goal, not yet the standard.

Use a timer. Actually use it. Resting "about a minute" by feel is usually 2–3 minutes, and that is how a 75-minute session becomes 2 hours. If a session is running long, cut the rest on the 40-second work — never on the 120-second work.

THE ORDER OF A MORNING BAR SESSION — fixed rule, 15 Sep. This never changes.

  1. Scapular pull-up — activation. Always first, always light.
  2. The hardest vertical pull (pull-up, or chin-up if there is no pull-up). Freshest muscle, because this is where reps and form actually matter.
  3. The second vertical pull if there is one.
  4. Horizontal pull (inverted row) — different plane, tolerates being tired.
  5. Negative pull-up — ALWAYS LAST of the pulling. Never before anything.
  6. Hanging knee raise, then dead hang to finish.

Why negatives go last: a 5-second negative is the most tissue-damaging thing in this whole programme. Put it first and every pull after it is performed on a muscle you have just deliberately torn down — which is exactly why your chin-ups failed on Days 1 and 4. Put it last and it does its job without taxing anything else.

Why pull-ups come before chin-ups: pull-ups are harder for you (max 5 against 7), so they get the fresh muscle. Chin-ups are supinated and biceps-heavy, so they also benefit from not being first in line behind an eccentric.

The one allowed superset: on upper days you may pair lateral raise + rear delt fly back-to-back with no rest between them, then rest 60 sec. They do not compete with each other and it saves you five minutes.

Weekly photos — the honest instrument

Your goal is visual. A scale infers body fat from an electrical estimate and carries real error on the absolute number. A photograph has zero measurement error for the thing you actually want. Over 100 days that is 14 data points on the real question.

Sunday morning. Fasted, after the bathroom, before water or food. Not after a session — a pump lies to you.

Four shots, every week, identical:
  1. Front, arms at your sides, completely relaxed
  2. Side, arms at your sides, relaxed
  3. Back, arms at your sides, relaxed
  4. Front, braced — one only

Shot 1 is the one that tells the truth. Most people only ever photograph themselves flexed, then cannot tell six weeks later whether anything changed. The relaxed front is your actual progress. The braced shot is for morale.

Identical every time or it is noise, not data: same room, same light (never a window — weather changes it), same distance, phone propped at navel height on the same surface, never handheld or tilted, same shorts.

Mark the floor with tape. Sounds excessive; it is the difference between a comparison and a guess.

Abs — thickness, not burn

Two different jobs, and you need both. Thickness is trained. Visibility is body fat. Nothing about being lean makes a flat muscle stand out — it just makes a flat muscle visible.

What was wrong with the old ab work: it was 20–40 rep bodyweight sets. That is endurance training. Endurance does not make a muscle thicker — if it did, every cyclist would have enormous legs. Your abs are a muscle like any other: they grow from load in the 10–15 rep range, progressed over time.

Three lifts now rotate through the fortnight:
  Weighted sit-up — the main one. Upper and mid rectus.
  Weighted reverse crunch — lower abs, your weakest ab region at 40 out of 100.
  Weighted Russian twist — obliques.
One stability piece stays each session — hollow hold or side plank. That is not thickness work; it keeps the middle stiff so the loaded lifts actually transfer.

THE LOAD LADDER, and it is the whole point:
  (1) 3 kg ball at the chest  →  (2) one 2.1 kg plate held straight overhead  →  (3) 3 kg ball held straight overhead  →  (4) both 2.1 kg plates overhead — 4.2 kg  →  (5) both 2.5 kg plates overhead — 5.0 kg  →  (6) both 2.7 kg plates overhead — 5.4 kg  →  (7) the 14.0 kg bell at your chest  →  (8) the 14.0 kg bell overhead. YOU ARE ON RUNG 3 (18 Sep) — two clean sessions holding the 3 kg ball straight overhead. Next is rung 4: both 2.1 kg plates overhead, 4.2 kg. Tell me when a rung gets easy and I move you up; never move yourself up mid-session.
Move up ONE rung only when you hit the top of the rep range on every set. Then the reps drop back down and you climb again. That is progressive overload, and it is the only reason a muscle gets bigger.

⚙️ HOW TO GET THE PLATES OFF WITHOUT BREAKING THE ONE-BELL RULE. Abs are the last loaded work of every session. So when you reach a rung that needs plates — rungs 4, 5 and 6 — you strip them off the bell at that point and only that point, and you do not put them back. The bell is finished for the day by then. Every finisher in this fortnight is bodyweight, so nothing after abs needs it. Always a matched pair, never a mixed combination — two 2.1 kg, or two 2.5 kg, or two 2.7 kg. One decision, not a puzzle.

The test: if you can do 25 reps, the weight is too light. Put something heavier in your hands. You are not trying to feel a burn — you are trying to make a lift hard.
